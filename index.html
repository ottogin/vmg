Guidance as Variance Matching

Ali Backour1*, Artem Lukoianov1*, Frank Permenter2, Tommi Jaakkola1, Justin Solomon1, Vincent Sitzmann1
1Massachusetts Institute of Technology  2Toyota Research Institute  * equal contribution
Paper (arXiv, coming soon) Code BibTeX
Key insight: we propose a closed-form rule that chooses the guidance scale so that the guided predictions match the data's variance; it recovers the scales used in practice across many models.
Our interpretation: each model makes a random error in predicting the posterior mean, and these errors scatter like a scaled-down copy of the posterior covariance. Guidance rescales the difference of two such errors into a sample with the posterior's covariance, which improves sampling.

TL;DR

Skip to How it works → The rule → Results → Citing

Classifier-free guidance and autoguidance push a diffusion model's prediction away from a weaker one, \(D_w = D_0 + w\,(D_1 - D_0)\). Everyone uses it, and the scale \(w\) is tuned by hand. We find that:

  • At the scales used in practice, guidance makes the prediction of the posterior mean worse, while samples get better.
  • Posterior-mean predictions have less variance than the data, and guidance restores it.
  • Picking \(w\) so that the guided predictions vary as much as the data recovers the published scales of eleven models across image, video, audio, motion and world generation, with no tuning.
  • Each model's error scatters like a scaled-down copy of the posterior covariance; guidance scales the difference of two such errors back up to a sample of the posterior.

The rule

Write the guided prediction as \(D_w = D_0 + w\,(D_1-D_0)\), where \(D_1\) is the primary prediction (conditional, or the strong model) and \(D_0\) the reference (unconditional, or the weak model). Over noisy versions \(x_t\) of a few hundred calibration examples, choose \(w\) so that the guided predictions vary as much as the clean data:

$$\operatorname{Var}_{x_t\sim p_t}\!\big[D_w(x_t)\big] \;=\; \operatorname{Var}_{x_0\sim p_{\text{data}}}\!\big[x_0\big].$$

The left side is quadratic in \(w\), so the scale has a closed form, per noise level or as one constant:

$$w^\star(t)=\frac{b(t)\pm\sqrt{b(t)^2-a(t)\,c(t)}}{a(t)},\qquad \begin{aligned} a&=\operatorname{Var}[D_1-D_0],\\ b&=\operatorname{Var}[D_0]-\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(D_0,D_1),\\ c&=\operatorname{Var}[D_0]-\operatorname{Var}[x_0]. \end{aligned}$$

It needs one pass of the two networks over the calibration inputs; nothing is sampled. When \(c<0\) the two roots have opposite signs, so the rule also predicts that guidance can work with a negative scale.
